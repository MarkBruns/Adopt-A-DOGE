# Dual-Path Multi-Agent Strategy: More Agentification For $1000

**Goal: Prove agentified USPTO data is the cheapest, fastest way to expand America’s Innovation Production Possibility Curve (IPPC) by tackling the 25 low-hanging improvements (semantic search, bulk modern formats, plain-English summaries, citation graphs, white-space mapping).** 

We focus on patent data because of the amount of work that has already been done to improve the knowledge engineering from patents. For example, we want AI to think about different agentics paths we might take to advance the brute-force origins of TRIZ. TRIZ, the Theory of Inventive Problem Solving, began in the Soviet Union in the late 1940s when Genrich Altshuller manually analyzed hundreds of thousands of patents. As a young patent inspector, he and colleague Raphael Shapiro along with help of their clerks and technicianssearched for repeatable patterns instead of treating inventions as unique events. They systematically classified solutions by the contradictions they resolved and extracted principles that worked across unrelated fields. TRIZ trained thousands of Soviet engineers and significantly aided technological development during the Cold War. TRIZ was so successful that it spawned official resistance by elements of the bureaucracy threatened by its significant impact upon scientific advances, with some labeling it “bourgeois pseudoscience.” Arrested in 1950 during Stalin’s purges and sentenced to the Gulag for his direct appealto Stalin, Altshuller was released in 1954 after Stalin’s death and although he had continued is work in the Gulag, when he was released, he resumed his work with renewed intensity and additional resources. Over subsequent decades, he and collaborators formalized TRIZ into tools such as the Contradiction Matrix with 40 Inventive Principles, ARIZ, and Laws of Technical Systems Evolution.

### Core Architecture: “OpenClaw + Chroma USPTO Swarm” (Dual-Path ... at first)

EVEN BEFORE Phase 0, we want to spend more time understanding our current proposed paths and the current landscape of vector databases and LLM inference frameworks ... how they can be combined in different ways to achieve our goals ... *but we also want to look at who might be doing something that is already several steps ahead of us.*

**Shared Layer**  
- **Vector Store**: Chroma (PersistentClient) — hybrid search + rich metadata (CPC, citations, examiner rejections, expiration, assignee).  
- **Embedding Model**: BGE/Snowflake-arctic on rented H200s → later distilled USPTO-specific embedder.  
- **Distilled Specialist LLM**: 7B–14B (or small-MoE) patent model via Kimi K2.5 teacher + Context-1 synthetic data.

**Path 1 — OpenClaw Multi-Agent Swarm** (maximum capability)  
- Orchestrator + Claw Agents (Data-Claw, Embed-Claw, Index-Claw, Claim-Eval Swarm) using OpenClaw routing.  
- Memory: OpenClaw shared + Chroma persistent.

**Path 2 — Chroma-Native Self-Editing Search Agent** (lighter, Context-1 inspired)  
- Single retrieval agent with multi-hop hybrid search + self-editing context pruning under token budget.  
- Downstream distilled LLM receives only the cleaned context — ultra-efficient on M4.

Both paths ship as one local Docker/MLX app.
